Silk-Lined Hood: Tailor Brief

Mulberry Empire, style MB-SOV-001 prototype. One hoodie (more to follow if it turns out well).

The job: line the hood of a finished heavyweight cotton hoodie with 100% silk charmeuse. The silk sits on the inside of the hood against the wearer's head and must not be visible from the outside. Everything else on the hoodie stays as it is.

What I'm bringing

HoodieLos Angeles Apparel 14 oz garment-dyed heavy fleece hoodie, black (already washed once, so it won't shrink further)
Silk1 yard of 100% mulberry silk charmeuse, 22 momme, 55" wide. Shiny (satin) side faces the wearer.

How it should be built

  1. Pattern: trace the existing hood panels (2 panels, center-back seam). Cut 2 silk panels the same shape, about 3 mm smaller at the crown so the silk doesn't bag. Add your normal seam allowance.
  2. Center-back seam: join the two silk panels with a French seam so there are no raw edges.
  3. Face opening: attach the silk about 2.5 cm (1") inside the hood edge, tucked under so no silk shows from the outside and it can't roll out. If there's a drawcord channel, sew below it so the drawcord still pulls freely.
  4. Neckline: stitch the silk into the neck seam and cover the seam with a black cotton twill tape (about 1/2") for a clean finish.
  5. Everywhere else the silk floats free inside the hood (not quilted or tacked to the cotton).

Sewing notes for the silk

  • Fine needle (Microtex 70/10), short stitch (14–16 per inch), light presser-foot pressure. Tissue paper under the silk helps stop puckering.
  • Iron on a low silk setting with a press cloth. No steam spotting.
  • Thread to match the silk colour (polyester or silk thread).

What I'll check when I pick it up

  • No silk visible from the outside with the hood up or down.
  • Center-back seam is flat with no raw edges and no puckering.
  • The hood still sits and drapes like before (not pulled tight or bunched).
  • Drawcord (if any) still works.
